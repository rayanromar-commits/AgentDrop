Good Luck Cutting Me out of the Yearbook.
subreddit: pettyrevenge
This is an old story from high school. Our yearbook came out mid-summer, so everyone brought theirs in at the start of the next school year for signatures. I'd forgotten to order my sophomore yearbook, so I didn't see it until the first week of junior year, when friends had me sign theirs.

To my surprise, there was a photo of me playing piano in the talent show — with a quote I definitely never gave. Something like, "I was really nervous before going on stage, but when I heard the applause, it was all worth it."

The dorky fake quote bugged me, but the lie of it bugged me more. So, still in my feelings about it, I went straight to the yearbook advisor. I told her it looked like her students were fabricating quotes. I hoped she'd promise to fix it. Instead, she made it clear she knew and approved, because it was "too hard and too much work to get quotes from teenagers."

As a parting shot, she told me, "If you don't want to be quoted in the yearbook, don't worry about it. You won't be in it."

I knew right then there'd never be another candid photo of me in my junior or senior yearbooks. And I was right.

But I realized something: she couldn't do anything about the posed, official photos. So my friends and I plotted to get me into every club photo possible. I was already in a lot of clubs. Others I just helped out with occasionally. And some advisors simply didn't notice an extra kid in the lineup — the members knew what I was doing and thought it was hilarious.

I ended up in dozens of club photos over my last two years. And every time, I made sure to wave at the yearbook advisor as I climbed the bleachers to my seat, sometimes with a polite "Hello, Ms. X."

I took a little joy every time I saw her looking at me like she'd just sucked a lemon.
